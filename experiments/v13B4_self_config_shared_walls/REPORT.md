# Neural Glyph v13B4 — Self-Configuring Fourth Face + Shared-Wall Service Framework

**Verdict: PARTIAL PASS — the fourth face can plausibly self-promote from repeated validated electrical use, and a 1.2 V contact-gate point preserves almost the full Tri-Wall exact/partial separation across nominal TT/FF/SS. Shared walls materially improve framework volume efficiency, but a floating shared capacitor wall is electrically dangerous and must be shielded. Leak/reset electricity should be divided into live-information, adaptation, and recovery phases rather than indiscriminately dumped to ground.**

## New terms

- **Self-Setting Fourth Face (S4F):** a fourth-face contact whose connection strength/state is learned from repeated validated local use instead of being permanently configured by software.
- **Use Reservoir:** a small slow analog charge state representing how often a particular fourth-face connection has recently produced a validated useful result.
- **Environment Reservoir:** a shared slow PVT/leak state generated by replica devices, reused from the v11U/v12A principle so raw route activity does not directly set the environmental threshold.
- **Janus Service Wall (JSW):** one structural wall shared by two neighboring cells, with electrically isolated capacitor faces on both sides and a robust middle shield/reference/service spine.
- **Validated Residual Tap:** a post-decision path that samples only a small part of otherwise-cleared charge into the Use Reservoir after a winner has already been validated.

## 1. Old v11/v12 ideas reused deliberately

This experiment does not invent a new control philosophy. It combines older mechanisms that already solved related problems.

### v11S — leaked charge as feedback

v11S explicitly tested not throwing leaked electricity away. Leak current charged a reservoir; as the reservoir voltage rose, it reduced future leak current. This corrected the SS/85C failure but became too sticky when ordinary activity was allowed to drive the state continuously.

Selected lesson: **leak charge is useful state, but ordinary activity must not indiscriminately write the adaptation state.**

### v11T/v11U — self-thresholding and PVT-only memory

v11T moved to a shared self-thresholding charge bucket. v11U separated the adaptation sensor from route activity using a tiny replica leak. The replica represented the slow statement “this region has been leaking too strongly recently.”

Selected lesson: **use a slow replica/environment state to set the local operating threshold, and keep fast route evidence separate.**

### v12A — two pilots for eight routers

v12A selected two pilot leak devices + one shared 5 pF analog state for eight routers rather than one sensor per neuron. In the preserved screen, the shared analog adaptation state was approximately 0 mV at FF, 16.38 mV at TT, and 50.53 mV mean at SS/85C.

Selected lesson: **the fourth-face threshold mechanism should be shared regionally, not duplicated on every face.**

### v12F — electricity directly changes threshold/inhibition

The electric homeostat used pooled firing charge directly to raise effective inhibition/threshold. Mean simultaneous firing fell from 1.767 to 1.429 cells while top-winner accuracy slightly improved in the stress model.

Selected lesson: **charge can directly tune excitability/connection policy without an ADC.**

### v12I / v11V/W — recycle after information is finished

v12I established a critical rule: while analog charge is still information, read it at high impedance and do not harvest it. After expiry, stale trace charge may be collected. The preserved expired-trace experiment captured about 68.9% of the removed trace energy into a low-voltage rail. Ambiguous membrane charge was also reused directly, retaining about 39.8–47.6% and reducing second-look latency by about 23–32%.

v11V/W separately showed one-way reset/clear collection into a shared fabric reservoir and then used recovered energy to support adaptation first.

Selected lesson: **live charge = information; controlled leak = adaptation; expired/reset charge = recovery.**

## 2. What is currently wasted in the v13B3 fourth-face bench

The v13B3 physical bench did not include an intentional recovery path for the Tri-Wall evidence cleanup or fourth-face gate discharge. In that bench, energy removed during cleanup ultimately returns through ideal sources/devices or is dissipated; it is not deliberately harvested.

Therefore the answer to “is leaked electricity being used?” is:

- in older v11/v12 recovery/adaptation experiments: often yes;
- in the present v13B3 fourth-face testbench: mostly no, unless the charge itself is still serving as the analog state;
- in the selected v13B4 architecture: it should be used in a staged life cycle rather than immediately dumped.

## 3. Charge life-cycle selected for the fourth face

### Phase A — live evidence

Three capacitor walls produce the local evidence. Do not load or harvest this state before the destination decision is captured.

### Phase B — validated residual

After a robust/local winner exists, a small fraction of the cleanup charge may be sampled into a local Use Reservoir.

Only **validated success** is allowed to do this. Raw events, noise, or an unvalidated partial response cannot train the contact. This reuses the v12P/Regional-Lease rule that only validated local success may refresh physical state.

### Phase C — expired surplus

Remaining cleanup charge is routed one-way toward the regional recovery rail, as in v11V/W and v12I, instead of being intentionally dumped to ground when practical.

Not all energy can be recovered. Switch resistance, leakage, conversion loss and parasitics still become heat.

## 4. New self-configuring contact architecture

The fourth face should not directly use the ~1 V Regional-Lease WAKE as its high-fidelity pass-gate voltage; v13B3 already rejected that because the pass NFET compressed a ~25 mV class difference to ~3.25 mV.

Instead the contact is trained slowly.

### Per fourth face

- one small Use Reservoir;
- one minimum pass MOS/contact;
- one tiny gated residual-tap path;
- no per-event ADC/read operation.

### Shared across one eight-way local group

- two replica PVT/leak pilots, following v12A;
- one shared Environment Reservoir/reference;
- one slow promotion/demotion decision element, which may reuse an existing regenerative reader because it runs only during configuration changes, not on every local event.

### State flow

`validated reuse -> residual charge sample -> Use Reservoir rises -> compare against PVT-tracking Environment threshold -> promote contact -> static/slow contact gate -> direct analog fourth-face transfer`

Inactivity reverses the process through controlled decay. A mid-level/unconfirmed contact remains in probation and continues using the closed robust reader/fallback path until promotion is validated.

This prevents a weak half-on contact from silently becoming the only correctness path.

## 5. Actual fourth-face gate-voltage experiment

The existing physical v13B3 topology was rerun with the real SKY130 MIM model, real minimum W=0.42/L=0.15 pass NFET, extracted local parasitics, 40 fF local state, and a 3 fF Contact Receptor.

The relevant result is that the pass gate does not need to stay at 1.8 V once we know where the transfer threshold is.

### TT sweep points

- 1.0 V gate: exact ~0.47995 V, partial ~0.47670 V -> only **3.25 mV** separation — FAIL for high-confidence transfer.
- 1.1 V gate: exact 0.513877 V, partial 0.493672 V -> **20.205 mV** separation.
- 1.2 V gate: exact 0.519761 V, partial 0.494680 V -> **25.082 mV** separation.
- 1.8 V gate: ~24.994 mV separation from the prior v13B3 run.

The transition is steep between 1.0 and 1.1 V. By 1.2 V the analog contact is already transferring almost the full class difference.

### Nominal PVT at 1.2 V

- TT: **25.082 mV** separation.
- FF: **25.059 mV** separation.
- SS: **25.064 mV** separation.

**Result: nominal TT/FF/SS PASS at 1.2 V.**

This is useful because a promoted contact does not necessarily need a permanent 1.8 V gate swing.

### First mismatch point at 1.2 V

One fresh `*_mm` launch per corner was also completed using the full PDK mismatch library:

- TT_mm: ~24.917 mV
- FF_mm: ~25.880 mV
- SS_mm: ~24.940 mV

All preserve the correct sign. This is only one launch per corner and is **not** a multi-seed mismatch signoff. The existing 1.8 V v13B3 screen remains the stronger mismatch reference (6/6 paired launches).

### Gate-charge proxy at 1.2 V

Measured absolute fourth-face gate source charge during the transfer window was about:

- TT: ~0.999 fC
- FF: ~0.984 fC
- SS: ~1.019 fC

Using gate voltage × charge only as a simple source-work proxy gives roughly **1.18–1.22 fJ**, below the prior ~1.5–1.65 fJ 1.8 V control cost.

This is not a full energy integral for the whole cell.

## 6. Self-threshold / self-promotion architecture model

This section is a model, not a new transistor-level signoff.

A 10 fF Use Reservoir begins around a 0.20 V low-voltage storage rail. A validated cleanup event is modeled as a limited residual-charge sample that moves the reservoir 20% toward an exact Tri-Wall evidence level of ~0.524 V. Inactivity removes about 6 mV × the old measured/effective leak factor per idle opportunity.

The PVT-dependent promotion reference is modeled using the old v12A shared pilot state:

- FF reference: 0.420 V
- TT reference: 0.4364 V
- SS reference: 0.4705 V

A ±20 mV hysteresis band prevents chatter.

### Repeated-use result

For consecutive validated uses:

- FF promotes after ~7 successes;
- TT after ~8;
- SS after ~11.

For alternating useful/idle opportunities:

- FF ~13 steps;
- TT ~15;
- SS ~27.

A sequence with only two initial valid successes followed by inactivity **never promotes**. Raw/noise-only activity also never promotes because it does not write the Use Reservoir.

This is the behavior wanted from a physical self-configuring connection: repeated useful relation -> stronger/static contact; isolated coincidences -> no promotion.

### Natural demotion model

Starting from a trained ~0.52 V Use Reservoir and then applying only inactivity:

- FF demotes after ~43 idle opportunities;
- TT after ~26;
- SS after ~15.

The faster SS demotion is intentional in this first model because the old leak sensor reported stronger hot/slow leakage there. Exact time constants must be chosen from a transistor-level retention experiment; these “steps” are not clock-time claims.

## 7. Can residual evidence actually fund the Use Reservoir?

Using the present ~72 fF physical evidence-node capacitance proxy:

- exact evidence ~0.524 V;
- baseline ~0.44 V;
- excess stored energy above baseline is roughly **2.9 fJ**;
- charge removed in an exact cleanup from 0.524 -> 0.44 V is roughly **6.0 fC**.

A 10 fF Use Reservoir needs only 0.5 fC for a 50 mV increment. That is roughly 8–10% of the available cleanup charge in this simple charge accounting.

Therefore a small post-capture residual tap is plausible energetically.

Important limitation: the ~0.5 V residual cannot directly create a >1.1 V pass-gate voltage. The low-voltage residual should be treated primarily as **information/configuration memory**, while a rare/shared level-restoring promotion circuit or VDD supplies the stronger contact-gate voltage. Recovered energy can offset that supply through the shared recovery rail, but voltage conversion is still required.

## 8. Shared walls — more detailed geometry screen

If every cell carries its own full shell, neighboring walls are duplicated. A shared framework lets one structural wall serve the two adjacent cells.

For wall-thickness/pitch ratio `t/p`:

| t/p | independent-shell solid | shared-framework solid | framework material saved | independent cavity | shared cavity | cavity gain | 3 capacitor-wall area / footprint, shared |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.02 | 11.5% | 5.9% | 49.0% | 88.5% | 94.1% | 6.4% | 2.88x |
| 0.05 | 27.1% | 14.3% | 47.4% | 72.9% | 85.7% | 17.6% | 2.71x |
| 0.10 | 48.8% | 27.1% | **44.5%** | 51.2% | **72.9%** | **42.4%** | **2.43x** |
| 0.20 | 78.4% | 48.8% | 37.8% | 21.6% | 51.2% | 137% | 1.92x |

These are ideal geometry ratios, not mechanical/fabrication measurements.

Using the user's illustrative 20 mm / 5 mm / 0.5 mm example (`t/p=0.1`):

- 4 x 4 x 4 = 64 cell pitches in the 20 mm cube;
- total geometric volume = 8000 mm^3;
- shared-framework solid volume ~2168 mm^3;
- cavity/utility volume ~5832 mm^3;
- independent shells would consume ~3904 mm^3 framework and leave ~4096 mm^3 cavity;
- sharing walls therefore saves ~1736 mm^3 of framework in this idealized model and creates ~42% more interior utility volume.

Again, these dimensions are only an analogy-scale geometry test.

## 9. Shared-wall electrical problem

A structural wall can be shared, but the two analog capacitor faces cannot simply share one floating middle conductor.

The v13A5 physical two-MIM series pair already gives the relevant warning: two legal 2x2 MIMs with a floating midpoint behave as about **4.756 fF** effective coupling at typical conditions.

If a neighboring cell switched 0.2 V through 4.756 fF into a ~72 fF evidence node, a first-order disturbance is about **12.4 mV**.

That is nearly half of the present ~25 mV useful Tri-Wall separation — unacceptable as the default wall.

## 10. Janus Service Wall — selected shared-wall solution

A shared wall should instead be built conceptually as:

`cell A capacitor face -> dielectric -> robust middle shield/service spine -> dielectric -> cell B capacitor face`

The middle spine must be low impedance or otherwise strongly referenced during analog evidence capture.

It can do several jobs simultaneously:

- electrostatic shield/reference for both capacitor faces;
- local ground/reference distribution;
- one-way recovery-bus collection after capture;
- thermal conduction path toward the outer package if thermally engineered;
- slow shared pilot/environment routing;
- mechanical structural wall.

This is the “building wall contains utilities” version of the user's framework analogy.

Using the present co-placed GC/GR direct coupling of ~0.135 fF only as a coupling proxy, a 0.2 V neighboring transition would inject about **0.37 mV** into a 72 fF node. A 0.05 fF target would be ~0.14 mV. These are much safer than the ~12.4 mV floating-middle case.

The exact Janus Wall geometry must still be physically laid out/extracted; the 0.135/0.05 fF values are not measurements of a future literal sidewall structure.

## 11. Selected architecture after v13B4

### Per cell / per contact

`3 capacitor walls -> local evidence -> small receptor/fourth-face pass MOS -> destination analog competition`

### Self-configuration

`validated result -> post-capture residual tap -> Use Reservoir -> slow regional PVT threshold -> promote/demote fourth face`

### Shared wall

`A capacitor face | dielectric | robust service/shield spine | dielectric | B capacitor face`

### Charge use

`live charge -> information`

`controlled leak -> adaptation / time / threshold`

`expired/reset charge -> one-way recovery rail`

### Robustness rule

Before promotion or whenever the use/reference margin is uncertain, continue using the closed v13A5/v13A6 robust reader or exact fallback. The self-configuring contact is an optimization, not a correctness dependency.

## 12. What remains unproven

- physical transistor-level Use Reservoir + two-pilot Environment Reservoir integration;
- independent multi-seed mismatch at the new 1.2 V pass-gate point;
- actual residual-charge tap efficiency at ~fF scale;
- retention/leak time constant of a compact Use Reservoir;
- physical Janus shared-wall extraction;
- thermal/mechanical feasibility of a real hollow silicon framework;
- whether shared wall service routing introduces more capacitance than it saves.

## 13. Next experiment

Build one **8-contact self-configuring local region** before attempting a true hollow package:

1. reuse one pair of v12A-style replica leak pilots for the eight contacts;
2. implement one local Use Reservoir + validated post-capture residual tap on at least two fourth faces;
3. use a shared slow promotion reader/trainer rather than eight per-event readers;
4. promote the contact to >=1.2 V only after repeated validated use;
5. run raw-noise, burst, repeated-use, inactivity and false-refresh batteries;
6. run TT/FF/SS + at least four independent mismatch launches per corner at the promoted gate voltage;
7. add one-way expired-charge recovery and measure whether it disturbs retention/evidence;
8. draw a planar proxy of one Janus Service Wall using two isolated MIM faces and a strongly referenced middle shield, then extract cross-coupling;
9. compare shared-wall area/capacitance against two fully separate cell walls;
10. only after these pass, attach the resulting contacts/Grammar/template/Myelin paths behind the physical eight-way Regional Lease.

The key new direction is not “make every connection adaptive.” It is: **stable useful relations may physically configure themselves from validated electrical history, while uncertain relations stay on the existing robust path.**
